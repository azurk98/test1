Sarajevo Population Grid

Estimated residents in every hexagonal cell of Centar, Stari Grad, Novo Sarajevo and Novi Grad. Each municipality adds up exactly to the official FZS figure; everything below that level is a model allocation from OSM buildings and WorldPop, so each cell also carries a range and a reliability grade.

  • GradesA: the cell's MZ total is constrained (Stari Grad from the MZ figures in its 2024–2027 strategy; Novi Grad from 2010 MZ records blended 50/50 with the model). B: only the municipality total is known. C: no mapped buildings, so WorldPop alone places people.
  • RangeMin and max across nine runs: building weight α = 0.5 / 0.7 / 0.9 against WorldPop, and estimated floor counts −1 / 0 / +1. The main estimate uses α per municipality: 0.9 in Novi Grad and Novo Sarajevo, 0.5 in Stari Grad, 0.7 in Centar.
  • CalibrationIn Novi Grad and Novo Sarajevo a square metre of house counts half as much as one in a block of 5+ floors, and WorldPop gets 10% of the weight, fitted to Novi Grad's 2010 MZ records. Stari Grad's old neighbourhoods fit best with no house correction and an even split with WorldPop: the MZ shares then differ from the municipality's own figures by 16% on average (30% with Novi Grad's settings). Centar has no MZ data and takes the midpoint.
  • Floor countsOnly 4.7% of OSM buildings record their floors. The rest come from a model trained on those that do (spatial cross-check: r = 0.70), since GHSL heights alone barely separate houses from towers (r = 0.13).
  • AttractivenessA location score from 0 to 100, separate from the population estimate. It weighs, as percentile ranks among populated cells: residents within a 10-minute walk (20%) and a 10-minute drive (15%), shops and services within a 10-minute walk (20%), stops within 400 m (15%), distance to the nearest stop (10%), distance to a main road (10%) and intersection density (10%). Walking and driving times run on the OSM street network. Walking slows on slopes (Tobler's hiking function: 4.8 km/h on the flat, 3.4 km/h up a 10% grade); driving assumes 65% of the speed limit, and transit counts stops only, without timetables. Cells at the edge toward Ilidža and Vogošća under-count reachable residents, since those municipalities are not modelled.
  • LivabilityWalking minutes, with slopes, from each cell to the nearest grocery, transit stop, primary school, clinic, park, kindergarten, pharmacy, playground, hospital and sports ground. Each counts half at a set time (5 min for a stop, 8 for a shop or park, 12 for a school or clinic, 25 for a hospital) and is weighted: grocery, transit and school 15% each, clinic and park 12%, kindergarten 8%, pharmacy 7%, playground 6%, hospital and sports 5%. Points come off near a motorway or primary road (up to 10), in industrial areas (10) and on very steep ground (5). Weights are a judgement, not a calibration. Places come from OpenStreetMap.
  • Car accessFor each cell, the drive along OSM streets from its centre to the nearest tertiary or larger road: the steepest 100 m on the way, metres of narrow road, and the share of the cell's own streets that are steep (over 12%) or narrow. OSM almost never records street width, so width is estimated as the gap between buildings on both sides; fences and walls are not mapped, so real streets can be narrower. Gradients come from the Copernicus surface model with buildings filtered out, measured over 100 m. Poor access takes up to 15 points off livability.
  • MZ outlinesNovi Grad's MZ outlines come from the official street list: buildings are matched by address, or by the nearest named street when that whole street belongs to one MZ (70% of homes), and the rest by their neighbours. Novo Sarajevo and Stari Grad outlines come from OpenStreetMap.

Supermarket sites

A Huff model splits each cell's grocery trips over the supermarkets within 20 minutes, weighted by store size and by travel time squared (walking with slopes taken into account, or driving plus two minutes to park). A new standard supermarket is placed in every cell that has none and lies within 400 m of a main road, and sites are picked one at a time so later picks compete with earlier ones. Customer figures are residents, not sales.

Existing stores come from OpenStreetMap plus 28 found only in Overture Maps Places. Comparing the two sources suggests they still hold only 35–60% of grocery stores, mostly missing small independent shops, so some gaps may already have a shop. Gap sites cluster in Novi Grad's suburbs, where the population model is least certain (it runs 1.75–2.5× above the 2010 MZ shares there) and where small shops are more likely to be missing from OpenStreetMap.
