Sarajevo Population Grid

Estimated residents in every hexagonal cell of Centar, Stari Grad, Novo Sarajevo and Novi Grad. Each municipality adds up exactly to the official FZS figure; everything below that level is a model allocation from OSM buildings and WorldPop, so each cell also carries a range and a reliability grade.

  • GradesA: the cell's MZ total is constrained (Novi Grad, from 2010 MZ records blended 50/50 with the model). B: only the municipality total is known. C: no mapped buildings, so WorldPop alone places people.
  • RangeMin and max across nine runs: building weight α = 0.5 / 0.7 / 0.9 against WorldPop, and estimated floor counts −1 / 0 / +1. The main estimate uses α = 0.9.
  • High-rise correctionTwo calibrations against Novi Grad's 2010 MZ records. A square metre of house counts half as much as one in a block of 5+ floors (houses hold about 100 m² per person, blocks about 50). WorldPop gets only 10% of the weight, since it puts 11% of Novi Grad's high-rise population where the 2010 records have 30%.
  • Floor countsOnly 4.7% of OSM buildings record their floors. The rest come from a model trained on those that do (spatial cross-check: r = 0.70), since GHSL heights alone barely separate houses from towers (r = 0.13).
  • AttractivenessA location score from 0 to 100, separate from the population estimate. It weighs, as percentile ranks among populated cells: residents within a 10-minute walk (20%) and a 10-minute drive (15%), shops and services within a 10-minute walk (20%), stops within 400 m (15%), distance to the nearest stop (10%), distance to a main road (10%) and intersection density (10%). Walking and driving times run on the OSM street network; driving assumes 65% of the speed limit, and transit counts stops only, without timetables. Cells at the edge toward Ilidža and Vogošća under-count reachable residents, since those municipalities are not modelled.
  • Still openStari Grad uses only its municipal total until the MZ names on Graf 3 of the 2024–2027 strategy are confirmed. Novi Grad MZ outlines are drawn from the 8% of buildings that carry an address, so some are rough: Dobrinja C is a sliver, which packs its MZ total into too few cells.

Supermarket sites

A Huff model splits each cell's grocery trips over the supermarkets within 20 minutes, weighted by store size and by travel time squared (walking, or driving plus two minutes to park). A new standard supermarket is placed in every cell that has none and lies within 400 m of a main road, and sites are picked one at a time so later picks compete with earlier ones. Customer figures are residents, not sales.

Comparing the two sources suggests they still hold only 35–60% of grocery stores, mostly missing small independent shops, so some gaps may already have a shop. Gap sites cluster in Novi Grad's suburbs, where the population model is least certain (it runs 1.75–2.5× above the 2010 MZ shares there) and where small shops are more likely to be missing from OpenStreetMap.
